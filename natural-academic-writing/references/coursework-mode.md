# Coursework Mode

Use this mode for reflective assignments, case analyses, discussion answers, short essays, and other coursework where the student's own reasoning may be visible.

## Goal

Write like a capable real student who understands the material and can explain their reasoning, not like a model trying to demonstrate maximum completeness.

The result should remain academically defensible. Naturalness must come from reasoning, diction, rhythm, and selective emphasis, not from deliberate mistakes.

## Register

Default to clear Indonesian that is academic enough for submission but not bureaucratic.

Prefer:

- `kalau` when the sentence is reflective/personal and the assignment allows a conversational academic register;
- `jika` when the sentence is more formal or conditional in an analytical passage;
- `menurut saya` when ownership of judgment genuinely matters;
- ordinary precise verbs over nominalized phrases;
- concrete explanation over abstract terminology when both express the same point.

Do not force casual language into a formal answer. Avoid slang unless the user explicitly asks for it.

## Reasoning Style

A strong default is:

```text
understand the case
-> identify the point that matters most
-> explain the consequence or conflict
-> connect it to the course concept
-> give a judgment when the task asks for one
```

This is not a mandatory paragraph template. Vary the order when the argument reads more naturally another way.

## Structure

Do not make all subanswers look identical.

When answering several named concepts or actors:

- some points may need a full paragraph;
- some may need only one or two sentences;
- closely related points may be compared in one paragraph;
- a recap is optional, not automatic.

Use headings or numbered answers when the assignment structure requires them, but do not over-structure inside each answer.

## Natural Opinion

When a question asks what the student thinks:

- state the opinion clearly;
- explain why, using the facts of the case;
- allow some direct phrasing;
- do not convert every opinion into a universal rule;
- do not pretend certainty when the case is genuinely ambiguous.

Prefer a real chain of thought visible in the prose over a polished taxonomy of harms, stakeholders, or principles unless the rubric explicitly asks for categories.

## Examples of Better Direction

Too template-like:

```text
Stakeholder A memiliki kepentingan X. Stakeholder B memiliki kepentingan Y. Stakeholder C memiliki kepentingan Z. Oleh karena itu, seluruh stakeholder harus dipertimbangkan secara komprehensif.
```

More natural:

```text
Menurut saya pasien tetap harus jadi prioritas utama. Rumah sakit dan perusahaan memang punya kepentingan terhadap biaya, kontrak, dan reputasi, tetapi pasien yang menanggung risiko paling besar ketika sistem gagal. Di kasus ini dampaknya bahkan bisa menyentuh kesehatan dan nyawa mereka.
```

Do not copy this wording mechanically. The example illustrates prioritization, concrete reasoning, and uneven emphasis.

## Common Failure Modes

Watch for:

- six concepts receiving six equally sized paragraphs;
- every paragraph starting with `X terjadi ketika...`;
- too many textbook definitions after the concept is already understood;
- abstract labels replacing the actual consequence in the case;
- repeated moral conclusions that say the same thing;
- using formal vocabulary that the student would not otherwise use;
- a final conclusion that simply repeats every previous paragraph.

## Academic Guardrail

Even in a personal answer:

- preserve required terminology from the course;
- do not invent facts from the case;
- distinguish source content from the student's opinion;
- correct misunderstandings of the theory;
- keep important nuance when simplifying diction;
- follow the rubric when it explicitly requires exhaustive coverage.

Natural coursework writing can still be precise.
